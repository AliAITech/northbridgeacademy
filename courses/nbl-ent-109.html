About This Course

What this course covers

This course introduces the practical foundations of starting a small business in Ontario. Participants explore problem selection, customer discovery, business models, market research, legal structures, registrations, pricing, bookkeeping awareness, sales, marketing, operations, risk, and launch planning. The course emphasizes responsible referral to lawyers, accountants, insurers, municipalities, and regulated professionals when specialized advice is required.

Learning Objectives

What you will learn

  • Evaluate a business idea using customer and market evidence.
  • Develop a clear value proposition and basic business model.
  • Identify common Ontario business structures, registrations, taxes, permits, and professional-advice needs.
  • Create basic pricing, sales, marketing, operations, and cash-flow assumptions.
  • Recognize startup risks, insurance considerations, and compliance boundaries.
  • Present a practical 90-day launch plan and business pitch.

Target Audience

Who this course is for

  • Newcomers and refugees considering self-employment.
  • Aspiring entrepreneurs and freelancers.
  • Owners of very small or home-based businesses.
  • Participants transitioning a skill into a legal, customer-focused business.

Delivery & Assessment

How the course is delivered and assessed

Delivery and learning methods: Instructor-led presentations, guided discussion, demonstrations, case studies, individual and group exercises, role-play or simulations, applied assignments, structured feedback, and a final practical or integrative activity appropriate to the subject area.

Assessment: Customer-discovery assignment 20%; business-model exercises 20%; financial and pricing worksheet 20%; marketing plan 15%; final pitch and 90-day launch plan 25%.

Certificate requirements: Minimum 80% attendance, completion of required learning activities and assessments, and a minimum overall grade of 70%. The certificate identifies the course title and scheduled duration and does not represent a licence, regulated credential, diploma, or guarantee of employment.

Required materials and technology: Internet-enabled device, spreadsheet and presentation software, notebook, and instructor templates.

Course Outline

Session by session

  1. Session 1Entrepreneurship in Ontario

    Entrepreneurial mindset, self-employment realities, opportunity identification, ethical business, and newcomer advantages and challenges.

    Learning objectives: Assess entrepreneurship readiness; identify opportunities; recognize common startup risks.

  2. Session 2Problem Selection and Customer Discovery

    Defining a real problem, customer interviews, assumptions, observation, and evidence-based validation.

    Learning objectives: Formulate customer hypotheses; conduct interviews; summarize evidence.

  3. Session 3Target Market and Competitive Research

    Customer segments, market size logic, competitor mapping, substitutes, online research, and avoiding unsupported claims.

    Learning objectives: Define a target segment; analyse competitors; identify a market gap.

  4. Session 4Value Proposition and Business Model

    Value proposition, channels, customer relationships, key activities, resources, partners, revenue, and costs.

    Learning objectives: Create a business-model canvas; articulate value; connect activities to revenue.

  5. Session 5Business Structures and Registration Awareness

    Sole proprietorship, partnership, corporation, business-name registration, CRA accounts, and when professional advice is needed.

    Learning objectives: Compare structures; identify registration steps; recognize advice boundaries.

  6. Session 6Licences, Permits, Zoning, and Insurance

    Municipal requirements, home-based business considerations, sector permits, liability, property and professional insurance awareness.

    Learning objectives: Identify possible permits; assess insurance needs; create a compliance checklist.

  7. Session 7Pricing and Unit Economics

    Cost identification, labour, materials, overhead, margins, competitor pricing, discounts, and break-even thinking.

    Learning objectives: Calculate basic costs and price; estimate contribution margin; avoid underpricing.

  8. Session 8Bookkeeping, Tax, and Cash-Flow Awareness

    Record keeping, invoices, receipts, sales tax awareness, business expenses, cash flow, and accountant referral.

    Learning objectives: Organize records; distinguish revenue and cash flow; identify tax-support needs.

  9. Session 9Brand Foundations and Market Positioning

    Business name, promise, audience, tone, trust signals, visual consistency, and ethical claims.

    Learning objectives: Define brand position; write a clear promise; identify trust-building elements.

  10. Session 10Digital Marketing and Lead Generation

    Website basics, social media, local search, content, referrals, email, community outreach, and campaign measurement.

    Learning objectives: Select channels; create a simple content plan; define lead metrics.

  11. Session 11Sales and Customer Conversations

    Discovery, proposals, quotations, objection handling, follow-up, deposits, and ethical selling.

    Learning objectives: Conduct a sales conversation; prepare a quotation; develop a follow-up process.

  12. Session 12Operations and Service Delivery

    Workflow, suppliers, scheduling, quality control, customer communication, documentation, and continuous improvement.

    Learning objectives: Map a service workflow; define quality checks; identify operational bottlenecks.

  13. Session 13Risk, Contracts, Privacy, and Consumer Trust

    Contract awareness, terms, cancellations, data privacy, complaints, refunds, and use of professional legal support.

    Learning objectives: Identify key business risks; establish basic policies; recognize legal referral needs.

  14. Session 14Growth, Partnerships, and Funding Readiness

    Organic growth, partnerships, grants and loans awareness, responsible borrowing, metrics, and capacity planning.

    Learning objectives: Evaluate growth options; identify potential partners; prepare funding information.

  15. Session 15Final Business Pitch and 90-Day Launch Plan

    Presentation of validated problem, customer, model, pricing, marketing, compliance checklist, milestones, and risks.

    Learning objectives: Deliver a concise pitch; defend assumptions; create a practical launch plan.

Scope & Limitations

Business education only. The course does not provide legal, accounting, tax, immigration, investment, or licensing advice and does not guarantee business success.
