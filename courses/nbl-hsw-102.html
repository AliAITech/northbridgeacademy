Home Support Worker – Intro Track

About This Course

What this course covers

This introductory course provides foundational knowledge for individuals interested in non-regulated homesupport and caregiving-assistant environments. Participants explore client-centred support, communication, personal care awareness, mobility principles, nutrition support, observation, documentation, infection prevention, safety, ethics, and professional boundaries. Practical demonstrations and simulations are included; however, the course does not qualify graduates as Personal Support Workers, healthcare professionals, or providers of controlled acts.

Learning Objectives

What you will learn

  • Describe the role, limits, and professional responsibilities of an entry-level home-support assistant.
  • Apply client-centred, dignity-preserving communication and support principles.
  • Demonstrate introductory safety, hygiene, mobility, and infection-prevention practices in simulations.
  • Recognize changes in client wellbeing and follow appropriate reporting and escalation procedures.
  • Maintain confidentiality, boundaries, respectful conduct, and accurate basic documentation.
  • Develop a personal pathway for further training or supervised employment in support services.

Target Audience

Who this course is for

  • Newcomers exploring entry-level support-service roles.
  • Individuals considering future PSW or community-support training.
  • Family caregivers seeking structured foundational knowledge.
  • Job seekers interested in supervised home-support environments.

Delivery & Assessment

How the course is delivered and assessed

Delivery and learning methods: Instructor-led presentations, guided discussion, demonstrations, case studies, individual and group exercises, role-play or simulations, applied assignments, structured feedback, and a final practical or integrative activity appropriate to the subject area.

Assessment: Knowledge checks 20%; participation and skills practice 30%; documentation and case exercises 20%; final practical simulation 30%.

Certificate requirements: Minimum 80% attendance, completion of required learning activities and assessments, and a minimum overall grade of 70%. The certificate identifies the course title and scheduled duration and does not represent a licence, regulated credential, diploma, or guarantee of employment.

Required materials and technology: Comfortable clothing for demonstrations, notebook, instructor handouts, basic personal protective equipment for training simulations, and access to online learning materials.

Course Outline

Session by session

  1. Session 1Introduction to Home Support Services

    Overview of home and community support in Ontario, common service settings, client-centred care, and differences between regulated and non-regulated roles.

    Learning objectives: Describe home-support settings; identify role limitations; explain client-centred support.

  2. Session 2Professional Responsibilities and Boundaries

    Reliability, scope of role, consent, privacy, boundaries, respectful conduct, and escalation when a request exceeds training.

    Learning objectives: Recognize professional boundaries; obtain consent; escalate out-of-scope requests.

  3. Session 3Understanding Client Needs and Care Directions

    Physical, social, emotional, cultural, and communication needs; reading basic care directions without independently creating clinical plans.

    Learning objectives: Identify categories of client need; follow written directions; distinguish observation from assessment.

  4. Session 4Communication and Relationship Building

    Active listening, empathy, respectful language, communication barriers, family interaction, and supporting client choice.

    Learning objectives: Build rapport; communicate respectfully; support autonomy and choice.

  5. Session 5Aging, Disability, and Person-Centred Support

    Common age-related changes, disability awareness, strengths-based language, accessibility, and avoiding stereotypes.

    Learning objectives: Recognize diverse support needs; use strengths-based language; promote inclusion.

  6. Session 6Personal Hygiene Support Awareness

    Dignity, privacy, preparation, infection prevention, and introductory simulation of safe assistance concepts without invasive procedures.

    Learning objectives: Explain hygiene-support principles; preserve dignity; identify infection risks.

  7. Session 7Dressing, Grooming, and Daily Routines

    Supporting independence with grooming, dressing, toileting awareness, routines, and respectful assistance.

    Learning objectives: Promote independence; organize safe routines; recognize when additional help is required.

  8. Session 8Body Mechanics and Injury Prevention

    Posture, centre of gravity, safe reaching, avoiding manual lifting beyond training, and use of assistance or equipment according to employer policy.

    Learning objectives: Demonstrate safe body mechanics; identify unsafe lifting; request appropriate assistance.

  9. Session 9Mobility Support and Fall Prevention

    Environmental checks, walking support awareness, fall-risk reduction, and response after a fall without unsafe movement.

    Learning objectives: Identify fall hazards; apply prevention strategies; follow safe response steps.

  10. Session 10Nutrition, Hydration, and Meal Support

    Basic nutrition and hydration, culturally appropriate meals, food safety, meal preparation boundaries, and observing swallowing concerns.

    Learning objectives: Support safe meals; recognize hydration concerns; report possible swallowing risks.

  11. Session 11Infection Prevention and Personal Protective Equipment

    Hand hygiene, respiratory etiquette, cleaning principles, PPE awareness, and reducing cross-contamination in home settings.

    Learning objectives: Perform effective hand hygiene; select basic PPE for simulations; reduce cross-contamination.

  12. Session 12Observation of Health and Wellbeing

    Noticing changes in appearance, mood, mobility, appetite, breathing, skin condition, and behaviour without diagnosing.

    Learning objectives: Observe objectively; recognize warning signs; report changes promptly.

  13. Session 13Documentation and Reporting

    Factual notes, incident reporting, privacy, communication logs, and timely escalation to supervisors or emergency services.

    Learning objectives: Write objective notes; complete basic reports; follow escalation pathways.

  14. Session 14Safety in the Home Environment

    Fire safety, trip hazards, chemicals, pets, smoking, equipment, lone-worker awareness, and personal safety.

    Learning objectives: Conduct a basic safety scan; identify hazards; follow personal-safety procedures.

  15. Session 15Emergency Awareness and Response Limits

    Recognizing urgent situations, calling 911, staying within training, documenting events, and the value of separate certified First Aid/CPR training.

    Learning objectives: Recognize emergencies; contact appropriate help; explain role limitations.

  16. Session 16Supporting Cognitive and Emotional Wellbeing

    Introductory awareness of dementia, confusion, loneliness, anxiety, and supportive communication without diagnosis or therapy.

    Learning objectives: Use supportive communication; recognize distress; refer concerns appropriately.

  17. Session 17Ethics, Privacy, and Cultural Safety

    Confidentiality, dignity, bias awareness, cultural humility, abuse and neglect awareness, and mandatory employer reporting processes.

    Learning objectives: Protect confidentiality; demonstrate cultural humility; recognize potential abuse indicators.

  18. Session 18Employment Readiness in Home Support

    Resume language, interview preparation, employer expectations, documentation checks, scheduling, and pathways to formal PSW education.

    Learning objectives: Present transferable skills; identify employer expectations; plan further qualifications.

  19. Session 19Final Practical Simulation and Development Plan

    Integrated scenarios involving communication, safety checks, meal support, observation, reporting, and professional boundaries.

    Learning objectives: Demonstrate safe introductory practices; integrate documentation and communication; create a development plan.

Scope & Limitations

Introductory only. This course is not a PSW diploma, does not authorize controlled acts, and does not replace regulated healthcare education, CPR, First Aid, or employer-specific training.
