Autism & ADHD Support Basics

About This Course

What this course covers

This course introduces neurodiversity-affirming, non-clinical support principles for working with children, youth, or adults who may have autism or ADHD. Participants learn respectful communication, sensory and environmental supports, routines, positive behaviour support, family collaboration, documentation, ethics, and referral boundaries. The course does not train participants to diagnose, treat, provide psychotherapy, or deliver regulated clinical interventions.

Learning Objectives

What you will learn

  • Explain neurodiversity using respectful, strengths-based language.
  • Recognize common communication, sensory, attention, and executive-function support needs.
  • Apply basic environmental, routine, and communication supports in non-clinical settings.
  • Use positive, preventative approaches to behaviour and emotional regulation.
  • Collaborate respectfully with families, educators, employers, and qualified professionals.
  • Maintain ethical boundaries, privacy, accurate observation, and appropriate referral practices.

Target Audience

Who this course is for

  • Parents and caregivers seeking foundational support knowledge.
  • Childcare, education, and community-support assistants.
  • Newcomers exploring support-oriented employment.
  • Individuals considering further education in behavioural or developmental services.

Delivery & Assessment

How the course is delivered and assessed

Delivery and learning methods: Instructor-led presentations, guided discussion, demonstrations, case studies, individual and group exercises, role-play or simulations, applied assignments, structured feedback, and a final practical or integrative activity appropriate to the subject area.

Assessment: Knowledge checks 20%; case-study exercises 25%; support-plan assignments 25%; participation 10%; final integrated case presentation 20%.

Certificate requirements: Minimum 80% attendance, completion of required learning activities and assessments, and a minimum overall grade of 70%. The certificate identifies the course title and scheduled duration and does not represent a licence, regulated credential, diploma, or guarantee of employment.

Required materials and technology: Instructor handouts, notebook, internet-enabled device, and access to document and presentation software.

Course Outline

Session by session

  1. Session 1Neurodiversity Foundations

    Neurodiversity, disability and social models, respectful terminology, individual differences, and strengths-based practice.

    Learning objectives: Define neurodiversity; use respectful language; distinguish support from normalization.

  2. Session 2Understanding Autism

    Core areas commonly associated with autism, variability across individuals, masking, strengths, and myths.

    Learning objectives: Recognize diverse autistic experiences; challenge myths; avoid stereotyping.

  3. Session 3Understanding ADHD

    Attention regulation, impulsivity, hyperactivity, executive-function differences, strengths, and common misconceptions.

    Learning objectives: Describe common ADHD-related needs; identify executive-function challenges; recognize strengths.

  4. Session 4Intersection and Co-occurrence

    Autism and ADHD together, learning differences, anxiety, trauma awareness, and the need for qualified assessment.

    Learning objectives: Recognize overlapping needs; avoid informal diagnosis; refer concerns appropriately.

  5. Session 5Communication Differences

    Literal language, processing time, non-speaking communication, active listening, visual communication, and respectful clarification.

    Learning objectives: Adapt communication; allow processing time; use visual or alternative supports.

  6. Session 6Sensory Processing and Environment

    Sensory seeking and avoidance, environmental triggers, sensory-friendly spaces, and individual preference.

    Learning objectives: Identify possible sensory factors; modify environments; respect sensory autonomy.

  7. Session 7Executive Function Support

    Planning, working memory, transitions, initiation, time awareness, and external supports.

    Learning objectives: Use checklists and prompts; support transitions; break tasks into manageable steps.

  8. Session 8Predictability, Routines, and Visual Supports

    Schedules, first-then tools, choice boards, preparation for change, and avoiding excessive rigidity.

    Learning objectives: Create simple visual supports; prepare for transitions; balance structure and flexibility.

  9. Session 9Understanding Behaviour as Communication

    Functions and context of behaviour, unmet needs, environmental factors, and objective observation.

    Learning objectives: Interpret behaviour contextually; identify possible unmet needs; record observations objectively.

  10. Session 10Positive and Preventative Support

    Antecedent adjustments, clear expectations, choices, reinforcement, skill building, and avoiding punitive responses.

    Learning objectives: Apply prevention strategies; use positive reinforcement ethically; support skill development.

  11. Session 11Emotional Regulation and Co-Regulation

    Recognizing escalation, calm adult presence, validation, coping options, and individualized regulation plans.

    Learning objectives: Recognize escalation signs; use co-regulation strategies; support safe coping.

  12. Session 12Responding to Distress and Meltdowns

    Reducing demands, safety, communication during distress, recovery time, and post-event review without blame.

    Learning objectives: Respond safely to distress; reduce escalation; conduct respectful post-event reflection.

  13. Session 13Supporting Learning and Participation

    Task adaptation, motivation, strengths, universal design, and collaborative goal setting.

    Learning objectives: Adapt tasks; increase participation; use strengths and interests.

  14. Session 14Family and Caregiver Collaboration

    Family expertise, cultural context, respectful questions, shared priorities, and communication boundaries.

    Learning objectives: Collaborate respectfully; recognize family expertise; maintain professional boundaries.

  15. Session 15School, Community, and Workplace Supports

    Environmental accommodations, clear instructions, task design, breaks, and inclusive participation across settings.

    Learning objectives: Identify practical accommodations; support inclusion; communicate support needs appropriately.

  16. Session 16Observation and Documentation

    Factual notes, patterns, context, confidentiality, and sharing information only with authorized persons.

    Learning objectives: Document objectively; protect privacy; identify patterns without diagnosing.

  17. Session 17Ethics, Consent, and Dignity

    Autonomy, assent, confidentiality, avoiding coercion, cultural humility, and rights-based support.

    Learning objectives: Respect autonomy; seek consent or assent; recognize unethical practices.

  18. Session 18Professional Boundaries and Referral

    Scope limits, red flags, crisis pathways, regulated professionals, and when to seek specialized support.

    Learning objectives: Maintain scope; identify referral needs; use appropriate escalation pathways.

  19. Session 19Final Case-Based Support Plan

    Development and presentation of a non-clinical, person-centred support plan integrating communication, environment, routine, and ethics.

    Learning objectives: Integrate course principles; justify selected supports; present an ethical support plan.

Scope & Limitations

Non-clinical introductory program. It does not qualify graduates to diagnose autism or ADHD, provide ABA therapy, psychotherapy, counselling, or regulated health services.
