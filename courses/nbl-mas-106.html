Massage Therapy Basics

About This Course

What this course covers

This introductory wellness course provides non-clinical foundations in client service, hygiene, body mechanics, relaxation-focused touch principles, contraindication awareness, consent, draping, documentation, and professional boundaries. Practical activities are limited to supervised, non-therapeutic wellness simulations. The program does not qualify participants as Registered Massage Therapists and does not authorize assessment, diagnosis, treatment, or representation as a regulated health professional.

Learning Objectives

What you will learn

  • Explain the difference between non-clinical wellness services and regulated massage therapy.
  • Maintain hygiene, consent, privacy, draping, and professional boundaries.
  • Use safe body mechanics and introductory relaxation-focused techniques in simulations.
  • Recognize contraindications, red flags, and circumstances requiring refusal or referral.
  • Conduct a basic non-clinical client intake and document services accurately.
  • Develop an ethical employment or further-education pathway.

Target Audience

Who this course is for

  • Individuals exploring spa and wellness assistant roles.
  • Newcomers considering further massage-therapy education.
  • Beauty and personal-service workers seeking foundational client-care skills.
  • Entrepreneurs exploring non-clinical wellness services within legal boundaries.

Delivery & Assessment

How the course is delivered and assessed

Delivery and learning methods: Instructor-led presentations, guided discussion, demonstrations, case studies, individual and group exercises, role-play or simulations, applied assignments, structured feedback, and a final practical or integrative activity appropriate to the subject area.

Assessment: Knowledge and safety checks 25%; participation and technique practice 30%; intake/documentation exercise 15%; final supervised wellness simulation 30%.

Certificate requirements: Minimum 80% attendance, completion of required learning activities and assessments, and a minimum overall grade of 70%. The certificate identifies the course title and scheduled duration and does not represent a licence, regulated credential, diploma, or guarantee of employment.

Required materials and technology: Comfortable clothing, linens or towels as instructed, personal hygiene supplies, notebook, and instructor-approved practice materials.

Course Outline

Session by session

  1. Session 1Wellness Services and Regulatory Boundaries

    Differences between spa wellness services and regulated massage therapy, protected titles, advertising boundaries, and further education.

    Learning objectives: Explain scope differences; recognize protected representations; identify further-training pathways.

  2. Session 2Professional Conduct, Consent, and Boundaries

    Informed consent, client choice, appropriate touch, privacy, boundaries, refusal of service, and complaint prevention.

    Learning objectives: Obtain consent; maintain boundaries; identify inappropriate requests.

  3. Session 3Hygiene and Infection Prevention

    Hand hygiene, linens, surfaces, product handling, cleaning schedules, and preventing cross-contamination.

    Learning objectives: Apply hygiene procedures; organize clean and used materials; reduce contamination risks.

  4. Session 4Body Mechanics and Practitioner Safety

    Posture, stance, weight transfer, table height, hand and wrist protection, pacing, and fatigue management.

    Learning objectives: Use safe body mechanics; adjust workspace; reduce repetitive-strain risks.

  5. Session 5Foundational Anatomy Awareness

    Major body regions, superficial muscle groups, joints, sensitive areas, and limits of non-clinical anatomical discussion.

    Learning objectives: Identify major body regions; recognize sensitive areas; avoid clinical claims.

  6. Session 6Client Intake and Service Planning

    Non-medical intake, goals for relaxation, preferences, allergies, medications as a referral flag, and documenting consent.

    Learning objectives: Conduct a basic intake; identify referral questions; document preferences accurately.

  7. Session 7Contraindications and Red Flags

    Fever, infection, acute injury, skin conditions, pregnancy considerations, unexplained pain, and when to postpone or refer.

    Learning objectives: Recognize common contraindications; refuse unsafe service; refer to qualified professionals.

  8. Session 8Draping, Positioning, and Client Comfort

    Privacy, secure draping, positioning supports, temperature, communication, and accessible setup.

    Learning objectives: Maintain privacy; position clients safely; monitor comfort.

  9. Session 9Introductory Relaxation Techniques I

    Supervised practice of broad, non-deep, non-clinical movements, pacing, pressure communication, and avoiding vulnerable areas.

    Learning objectives: Demonstrate introductory techniques; communicate pressure; maintain safe limits.

  10. Session 10Introductory Relaxation Techniques II

    Sequencing a short relaxation service, transitions, rhythm, product use, and hygiene during practice.

    Learning objectives: Sequence a basic service; maintain continuity; use products safely.

  11. Session 11Chair-Based Wellness Service Awareness

    Ergonomic setup, brief seated services, clothing-on techniques, event settings, and consent.

    Learning objectives: Set up a chair-based service; maintain boundaries; adapt to public settings.

  12. Session 12Customer Service and Inclusive Practice

    Welcoming clients, trauma-aware communication, cultural humility, accessibility, and handling dissatisfaction.

    Learning objectives: Communicate inclusively; respond to concerns; maintain client dignity.

  13. Session 13Records, Payments, and Privacy

    Service notes, consent records, scheduling, cancellation policies, receipts, privacy, and secure storage.

    Learning objectives: Complete basic records; protect client information; explain service policies.

  14. Session 14Employment, Insurance, and Business Boundaries

    Spa roles, supervision, liability insurance awareness, municipal requirements, marketing claims, and further credentials.

    Learning objectives: Identify employment pathways; recognize insurance needs; avoid misleading claims.

  15. Session 15Final Supervised Wellness Simulation

    Complete intake, consent, setup, short relaxation sequence, after-service communication, cleaning, and self-evaluation.

    Learning objectives: Integrate safe service steps; demonstrate professionalism; develop an improvement plan.

Scope & Limitations

Non-clinical and introductory only. Graduates must not use the title RMT, diagnose or treat conditions, or provide services outside applicable law and insurance requirements.
