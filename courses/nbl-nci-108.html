Newcomer Integration Package

Program Code: NBL-NCI-108
Duration20 scheduled hours
Sessions10 sessions × 2 hours (1 hour 45 minutes of instruction and practice, plus a 15-minute break)
Delivery ModeOnline / Hybrid
Course TypeShort Course / Practical
CredentialNorthBridge Academy Certificate of Completion

About This Course

What this course covers

This practical orientation course supports newcomers and refugees in navigating everyday life, employment systems, public services, financial basics, rights and responsibilities, communication, housing, healthcare access, education pathways, and community participation in Ontario. The course emphasizes reliable information sources, personal action planning, and appropriate referrals rather than legal, immigration, tax, or financial advice.

Learning Objectives

What you will learn

  • Navigate key settlement, employment, healthcare, education, banking, housing, and community systems.
  • Identify reliable government and community information sources.
  • Understand foundational workplace rights, responsibilities, and cultural expectations.
  • Build practical communication, budgeting, and service-access skills.
  • Recognize when professional legal, immigration, tax, financial, or health advice is required.
  • Create a personal 90-day settlement and integration plan.

Target Audience

Who this course is for

  • Newcomers, refugees, protected persons, and recently arrived families.
  • Internationally trained individuals adapting to Ontario systems.
  • Job seekers needing a structured settlement orientation.
  • Community members supporting newly arrived relatives.

Delivery & Assessment

How the course is delivered and assessed

Delivery and learning methods: Instructor-led presentations, guided discussion, demonstrations, case studies, individual and group exercises, role-play or simulations, applied assignments, structured feedback, and a final practical or integrative activity appropriate to the subject area.

Assessment: Participation 20%; service-navigation exercises 25%; budgeting and employment tasks 25%; final 90-day integration plan 30%.

Certificate requirements: Minimum 80% attendance, completion of required learning activities and assessments, and a minimum overall grade of 70%. The certificate identifies the course title and scheduled duration and does not represent a licence, regulated credential, diploma, or guarantee of employment.

Required materials and technology: Internet-enabled device, notebook, instructor resource guide, and access to government and community websites.

Course Outline

Session by session

  1. Session 1Orientation to Ontario and Reliable Information

    Levels of government, official information sources, identification documents, settlement agencies, language services, and avoiding misinformation.

    Learning objectives: Identify government levels; locate reliable sources; create a personal document checklist.

  2. Session 2Housing, Transportation, and Daily Services

    Rental-search basics, tenant information sources, utilities, public transit, libraries, recreation, and emergency contacts.

    Learning objectives: Navigate daily services; identify housing resources; plan local transportation.

  3. Session 3Healthcare and Community Wellbeing

    Health card awareness, primary care, walk-in and emergency services, pharmacies, mental-health resources, and when to call 911.

    Learning objectives: Distinguish healthcare options; locate community supports; recognize emergencies.

  4. Session 4Education, Childcare, and Family Services

    School registration, adult learning, credential pathways, childcare information, libraries, and family supports.

    Learning objectives: Identify education pathways; locate childcare resources; plan family service access.

  5. Session 5Employment System and Canadian Workplace Culture

    Job-search channels, resumes, interviews, transferable skills, workplace communication, punctuality, inclusion, and feedback.

    Learning objectives: Navigate job-search channels; describe workplace expectations; identify transferable skills.

  6. Session 6Employment Rights, Responsibilities, and Safety

    Employment standards information, pay statements, scheduling, workplace safety, harassment resources, and documenting concerns.

    Learning objectives: Locate official employment information; recognize basic workplace rights; document concerns appropriately.

  7. Session 7Banking, Credit, Budgeting, and Consumer Awareness

    Bank accounts, debit and credit, credit history, budgeting, fraud awareness, contracts, and consumer protection resources.

    Learning objectives: Use basic banking services; create a budget; identify common fraud risks.

  8. Session 8Taxes, Benefits, and Professional Referral

    Tax filing awareness, benefits and credits, record keeping, and when to use a qualified tax or financial professional.

    Learning objectives: Identify tax-season documents; locate official benefit information; recognize advice boundaries.

  9. Session 9Communication, Community, and Networking

    Professional communication, community participation, volunteering, networking, cultural adaptation, and maintaining wellbeing.

    Learning objectives: Build a support network; practice professional introductions; identify community participation options.

  10. Session 10Final 90-Day Integration Plan

    Participants create a prioritized plan covering employment, language, housing, health, finance, education, and community connections.

    Learning objectives: Set realistic goals; identify resources and deadlines; present a practical integration plan.

Scope & Limitations

General educational information only. The course does not provide legal, immigration, tax, financial, medical, or mental-health advice.
