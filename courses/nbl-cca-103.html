About This Course

What this course covers

This introductory course provides foundational knowledge for individuals interested in supervised childcare assistant environments. Participants study child development, play-based learning, communication, observation, health and safety, inclusion, behaviour guidance, family relationships, professionalism, and activity planning. The program supports employment readiness but does not confer Early Childhood Educator status, ECE registration, or authorization to operate a childcare program.

Learning Objectives

What you will learn

  • Describe basic stages and domains of child development.
  • Support safe, inclusive, play-based activities under appropriate supervision.
  • Use positive, age-appropriate communication and behaviour-guidance strategies.
  • Observe and document children’s participation objectively and respectfully.
  • Communicate professionally with families and childcare teams within role boundaries.
  • Recognize safety, safeguarding, privacy, and escalation responsibilities.

Target Audience

Who this course is for

  • Newcomers exploring supervised childcare-support roles.
  • Individuals considering further ECA or ECE education.
  • Parents and community workers seeking foundational childcare knowledge.
  • Job seekers interested in early-learning environments.

Delivery & Assessment

How the course is delivered and assessed

Delivery and learning methods: Instructor-led presentations, guided discussion, demonstrations, case studies, individual and group exercises, role-play or simulations, applied assignments, structured feedback, and a final practical or integrative activity appropriate to the subject area.

Assessment: Knowledge checks 20%; activity-planning assignments 20%; observation and documentation exercises 20%; participation and simulations 20%; final practical project 20%.

Certificate requirements: Minimum 80% attendance, completion of required learning activities and assessments, and a minimum overall grade of 70%. The certificate identifies the course title and scheduled duration and does not represent a licence, regulated credential, diploma, or guarantee of employment.

Required materials and technology: Notebook, instructor handouts, basic craft materials, access to a digital device, and comfortable clothing for activity demonstrations.

Course Outline

Session by session

  1. Session 1Introduction to Childcare Environments

    Types of childcare settings, roles within early-learning teams, supervised assistant responsibilities, and professional expectations.

    Learning objectives: Identify childcare settings; describe assistant responsibilities; recognize role boundaries.

  2. Session 2Foundations of Child Development

    Physical, cognitive, language, social, and emotional development from infancy through early childhood.

    Learning objectives: Describe development domains; recognize individual variation; connect activities to development.

  3. Session 3Developmental Milestones and Individual Differences

    Typical milestone ranges, cultural and family context, observation without diagnosis, and referral to qualified professionals.

    Learning objectives: Recognize milestone patterns; avoid inappropriate diagnosis; report concerns through proper channels.

  4. Session 4Play-Based Learning

    Value of play, types of play, adult facilitation, open-ended materials, and learning through exploration.

    Learning objectives: Explain play-based learning; facilitate child-led play; select open-ended materials.

  5. Session 5Designing Age-Appropriate Activities

    Planning art, sensory, language, movement, and early numeracy activities suited to developmental level and interests.

    Learning objectives: Plan age-appropriate activities; define learning purpose; adapt activities for engagement.

  6. Session 6Safe Supervision Practices

    Active supervision, positioning, scanning, transitions, attendance awareness, and preventing common incidents.

    Learning objectives: Apply active-supervision principles; identify high-risk transitions; maintain awareness of children.

  7. Session 7Healthy Environments and Infection Prevention

    Hand hygiene, cleaning routines, illness awareness, respiratory etiquette, and reducing transmission in group settings.

    Learning objectives: Support hygiene routines; identify contamination risks; follow illness-reporting procedures.

  8. Session 8Nutrition, Allergies, and Mealtime Support

    Healthy food awareness, cultural considerations, allergy precautions, choking-risk awareness, and role limits.

    Learning objectives: Promote positive mealtimes; recognize allergy precautions; follow emergency plans and role limits.

  9. Session 9Positive Communication with Children

    Listening, clear instructions, encouraging language, choices, emotional validation, and age-appropriate explanations.

    Learning objectives: Use positive language; give clear instructions; support emotional expression.

  10. Session 10Positive Behaviour Guidance

    Understanding behaviour as communication, prevention strategies, routines, redirection, logical limits, and avoiding punitive practices.

    Learning objectives: Interpret possible behaviour needs; apply positive guidance; maintain calm boundaries.

  11. Session 11Inclusive Practice and Accessibility

    Welcoming diverse abilities, cultures, languages, and family structures; adapting activities and environments.

    Learning objectives: Use inclusive language; adapt activities; support participation of diverse learners.

  12. Session 12Introduction to Neurodiversity in Childcare

    Foundational awareness of sensory, communication, attention, and regulation differences; collaboration with qualified staff and families.

    Learning objectives: Recognize diverse support needs; apply simple inclusive supports; avoid diagnosis and labelling.

  13. Session 13Observation Skills

    Objective observation, anecdotal notes, confidentiality, noticing interests and participation, and separating facts from interpretation.

    Learning objectives: Conduct objective observations; record factual notes; protect confidentiality.

  14. Session 14Documentation and Activity Reflection

    Basic documentation, evaluating activity effectiveness, reflecting on participation, and communicating observations to supervisors.

    Learning objectives: Complete simple documentation; evaluate an activity; communicate observations professionally.

  15. Session 15Working with Families

    Respectful greetings, information sharing within authorization, cultural humility, boundaries, and handling concerns through supervisors.

    Learning objectives: Build respectful relationships; maintain boundaries; route concerns appropriately.

  16. Session 16Teamwork in Early-Learning Settings

    Roles, shift communication, preparation, transitions, receiving feedback, and supporting educators consistently.

    Learning objectives: Collaborate with teams; communicate during transitions; respond to feedback.

  17. Session 17Safeguarding, Privacy, and Professional Conduct

    Child protection awareness, confidentiality, appropriate touch, digital privacy, social media boundaries, and employer policies.

    Learning objectives: Recognize safeguarding concerns; protect privacy; demonstrate professional conduct.

  18. Session 18Employment Readiness and Further Education

    Resume preparation, interview scenarios, screening requirements, workplace expectations, and ECA/ECE education pathways.

    Learning objectives: Prepare for entry-level interviews; identify required checks; plan further education.

  19. Session 19Final Activity Plan and Childcare Simulation

    Participants deliver an age-appropriate activity plan and respond to simulated supervision, communication, and behaviour-guidance scenarios.

    Learning objectives: Deliver a safe activity; integrate positive guidance; reflect on performance and next steps.

Scope & Limitations

Introductory only. This course is not an ECE diploma, does not provide registration with the College of Early Childhood Educators, and does not replace employer-required screening, First Aid/CPR, or legislated training.
