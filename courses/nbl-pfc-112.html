About This Course

What this course covers

This course builds practical personal-finance and record-keeping skills for individuals, newcomers, employees, and self-employed workers in Canada. Topics include banking, budgeting, credit, debt, taxes, benefits, insurance awareness, saving, investing concepts, fraud prevention, self-employment records, and financial planning. The program is educational and does not provide personalized tax, legal, accounting, investment, or credit advice.

Learning Objectives

What you will learn

  • Create and maintain a practical household budget and financial record system.
  • Use Canadian banking and credit products more confidently and responsibly.
  • Recognize foundational tax documents, filing responsibilities, benefits, and professional-advice needs.
  • Organize records for employment, household, or basic self-employment purposes.
  • Understand introductory saving, insurance, debt, and investment concepts.
  • Develop a realistic personal financial action plan.

Target Audience

Who this course is for

  • Newcomers adapting to Canadian financial systems.
  • Employees, students, and families seeking stronger financial literacy.
  • Freelancers and very small self-employed operators.
  • Individuals preparing to work with qualified tax or financial professionals.

Delivery & Assessment

How the course is delivered and assessed

Delivery and learning methods: Instructor-led presentations, guided discussion, demonstrations, case studies, individual and group exercises, role-play or simulations, applied assignments, structured feedback, and a final practical or integrative activity appropriate to the subject area.

Assessment: Budgeting and record-keeping assignments 30%; credit and tax exercises 20%; knowledge checks 20%; final personal financial plan 30%.

Certificate requirements: Minimum 80% attendance, completion of required learning activities and assessments, and a minimum overall grade of 70%. The certificate identifies the course title and scheduled duration and does not represent a licence, regulated credential, diploma, or guarantee of employment.

Required materials and technology: Calculator, spreadsheet software, internet-enabled device, notebook, and sample financial forms supplied by the instructor.

Course Outline

Session by session

  1. Session 1Canadian Financial System Overview

    Banks, credit unions, payment methods, financial institutions, consumer protection, and reliable information sources.

    Learning objectives: Identify financial institutions; compare service types; locate reliable information.

  2. Session 2Bank Accounts and Everyday Transactions

    Chequing and savings accounts, fees, holds, direct deposit, e-transfers, statements, and fraud precautions.

    Learning objectives: Compare accounts; read a bank statement; use transaction services safely.

  3. Session 3Budgeting Foundations

    Income, fixed and variable expenses, needs and wants, irregular costs, and zero-based or category budgeting.

    Learning objectives: Build a monthly budget; classify expenses; plan for irregular costs.

  4. Session 4Cash-Flow and Emergency Planning

    Timing of income and payments, cash-flow calendars, emergency funds, shortfalls, and prioritization.

    Learning objectives: Create a cash-flow calendar; set an emergency-fund target; prioritize essential payments.

  5. Session 5Credit Reports and Scores

    Credit bureaus, factors affecting scores, checking reports, disputes, building history, and protecting identity.

    Learning objectives: Explain credit-score factors; review a sample report; plan responsible credit building.

  6. Session 6Credit Cards, Loans, and Cost of Borrowing

    Interest, minimum payments, amortization awareness, lines of credit, payday loans, and debt warning signs.

    Learning objectives: Compare borrowing costs; identify high-cost debt; calculate simple interest impacts.

  7. Session 7Debt Management and Consumer Options

    Debt inventory, repayment strategies, negotiating, reputable counselling, insolvency-professional referral, and scam avoidance.

    Learning objectives: Create a debt summary; compare repayment methods; identify qualified support.

  8. Session 8Income Taxes in Canada

    Tax year, residency awareness, T4/T5 slips, deductions and credits, filing methods, deadlines, and professional referral.

    Learning objectives: Identify common tax documents; explain basic filing steps; recognize advice boundaries.

  9. Session 9Benefits, Credits, and Government Accounts

    Common benefit categories, official online accounts, direct deposit, keeping information updated, and avoiding benefit scams.

    Learning objectives: Locate official benefit information; organize account access; protect personal data.

  10. Session 10Self-Employment Record Keeping

    Invoices, receipts, mileage and expense logs, separating business and personal funds, HST awareness, and accountant support.

    Learning objectives: Organize self-employment records; create an invoice; identify tax-support needs.

  11. Session 11Saving Goals and Registered Accounts Awareness

    Short-, medium-, and long-term goals; TFSA, RRSP, RESP awareness; liquidity, risk, and contribution limit verification.

    Learning objectives: Set saving goals; distinguish account purposes; verify current rules before decisions.

  12. Session 12Investment Foundations and Risk

    Stocks, bonds, funds, diversification, fees, risk tolerance, fraud, and working with registered professionals.

    Learning objectives: Explain basic asset types; recognize risk and fees; identify investment scams.

  13. Session 13Insurance and Financial Risk Awareness

    Tenant, auto, life, disability, health, and business insurance concepts; deductibles, exclusions, and licensed advice.

    Learning objectives: Describe insurance purposes; read basic policy terms; identify advice needs.

  14. Session 14Fraud, Identity Theft, and Financial Safety

    Phishing, impersonation, investment fraud, job scams, romance scams, reporting, and account security.

    Learning objectives: Recognize scam indicators; secure accounts; follow reporting steps.

  15. Session 15Final Personal Financial Action Plan

    Integrated budget, debt or saving priorities, record system, tax checklist, risk controls, and 90-day action plan.

    Learning objectives: Integrate course tools; prioritize actions; identify professional support when required.

Scope & Limitations

General education only. The course does not provide individualized tax, accounting, legal, credit-repair, insurance, or investment advice.
